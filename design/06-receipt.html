Sample 06 · thermal output · spec 11

The receipt is not a screen

Every other sample is pixels, colour, theme states and touch targets. This one is 32 or 48 fixed characters burned into a roll of paper — no colour, no images, no layout engine, no second chance. It is here to show where the design system stops.

What this is. Four real print jobs, previewed at their exact character widths: a 32-column customer receipt, a 48-column kitchen ticket, the same receipt reprinted as a COPY, and the VOID ticket that a voided SENT item sends to the kitchen. The rule it demonstrates is a boundary. None of the screen tokens apply here (design-system.md §8). Paper is --c-raise, ink is --c-ink, and that is the entire palette — every other token on this page belongs to the chrome around the paper, never to the paper itself.

Two widths, and you have to count them

58 mm prints 32 characters. 80 mm prints 48.

There is nothing in between and there is no reflow. A line one character too long wraps, and a wrapped total is a phone call on a Friday night. So the layout is planned in characters, previewed in --font-mono at the target width, and carries a column ruler above it so the width is provable rather than asserted. Both tapes below are generated from field widths, with every rule line asserted at full width — which is why the paper is exactly as wide as the receipt is.

Reprints

A reprint says COPY, and that is not a courtesy

Spec 11 requires the mark, and the reason is accounting rather than politeness: a second piece of paper that looks identical to the first is a second receipt for one sale. The reprint takes no new invoice number, records no payment, posts no journal entry and does not kick the drawer. It is a print job the agent replays, not a transaction (invariants 2 and 4).

Which is why the banner sits at the top, in the widest mark the printer has, above the restaurant name — a customer holding the paper at arm's length sees it before anything else, and it cannot be torn off without taking the totals with it. The two timestamps do the rest of the work: Sold and Reprinted are separate lines, because collapsed into one the copy would be indistinguishable from a sale made at 20:05.

The other exception ticket

A voided SENT item prints a VOID ticket

Once an item is ▲SENT, removing it is a void, never a delete (spec 13). The kitchen already holds the first ticket, and the only channel that reaches it is a second one. So the void is not a screen event that quietly disappears a line — it is a piece of paper that has to arrive at the pass before the food does.

The ticket carries the reason code and the approver because both are mandatory and both are already sitting in the audit row written inside the same transaction (invariants 9 and 10). Deleting a ◇NEW item prints nothing at all, needs no reason and needs no approval — nobody downstream ever heard about it.

The boundary

The same number, in two media

This is the honest version of “none of the screen tokens apply”. Each row is one piece of information the product has to render twice, and the paper column is always the poorer of the two — which is what makes it the useful test of a rule.

How four pieces of information render on screen and on thermal paper.
What On screen On paper
The order total $37.80 .money-total · --font-mono · tabular-nums · right-aligned TOTAL at column 1, $37.80 right-aligned to column 32, fenced by two = rules. Emphasis is double height, because there is only ever one size.
A discount −2.00 — U+2212 and --c-danger, per design-system.md §6. Never colour alone. -2.00 — an ASCII hyphen. CP437 has no U+2212, and the printer has no colour at all, so the sign carries the entire meaning by itself.
Item status ▲ SENT ✕ VOIDED glyph, plus colour, plus a strike-through for a void. The word — or, for a void, an entire separate ticket. ◇ ▲ ✕ ● ◆ are a screen vocabulary; the printer's code page cannot be relied on for any of them.
Everything else Twelve surface tokens across three theme states, a four-step touch scale, three type families, elevation, focus rings. One ink. One paper. One monospaced face. A fixed grid of 32 or 48 columns.

The discount row is the one worth arguing about. On screen the rule is a leading − and --c-danger — belt and braces, because roughly one man in twelve has red-green colour vision deficiency and a counter screen is read under glare. On paper, half of that rule is simply unavailable. That is not the rule breaking; it is the reason the rule is written the way it is. A design that had leaned on the red would have had nothing left to print.

Before this ships

Two things on this paper are not decided

Surfaced rather than quietly baked in, per the house rule. Both of them change the tape, and one of them changes the chart of accounts.

Spec 33 · decision 3 — open

Legal receipt requirements

The sample prints VAT 5% (exclusive) and a Taxable line, and carries no tax registration number, no legally mandated wording and no fiscal signature — because nobody has confirmed what is required locally. Working default: tax mode is a restaurant setting read at calculation time, rounded once on the invoice total. Confirm with a local accountant before the header block is treated as final.

Not in the spec at all — open

A tip line, or a service charge

Neither appears above, and that is a choice rather than an oversight. A tip line needs a liability account and a payout path; a service charge is revenue, and is taxed. Either adds rows to the chart of accounts, so neither gets invented inside a receipt mock-up. Proposed default: ship with neither, and treat a request for one as a new decision.
