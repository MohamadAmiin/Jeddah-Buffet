Sample 03 · auth

Two doors, one split

Everything a person can reach without a session: the sign-in page — shown with the throttle's rejection in place — and the one-time registration that brings the restaurant into existence. Each is a split: the form on one side, and on the other the one thing this product is actually about.

What this demonstrates. One split grammar serving a 2-field form and a 7-field one. The form half is --c-raise, vertically centred, capped at --container-form; the brand half is --c-accent with --c-accent-ink at 6.13:1, and it is made of type and tokens only — no photograph, no <img>, no gradient. Below 864px the split becomes one column and the brand half moves above the form, by order — so the DOM keeps the form first and the email field is still the first tab stop. Press Tab to walk the page and watch the 2px --c-ring outline land on every control; the failure carries a glyph and words, never colour alone. Toggle the theme — no value here is written twice.

Why it is built this way

Five decisions in this split

layout · 54rem

The brand half goes on top, not underneath

Stacked, the smaller panel leads and the form follows — a form pushed below a full screen of marketing is a form nobody reaches. The move is order, not source order, so the DOM still reads form-then-brand and the email field is the first tab stop at every width.

spec 7 · house rule

Why the message names a number

Five wrong PINs lock a POS employee out for five minutes. Applied literally to a page anyone on the internet can reach, that rule becomes a lever for locking the owner out of their own restaurant — so this door pairs a per-account delay with a per-IP throttle in front of the password hash, and says how long it has left rather than leaving the owner to guess.

first run only

The door closes behind itself

/register answers only while zero restaurants exist and the token matches SETUP_TOKEN. No environment variable re-opens it and no flag resets it; more restaurants are made on the server with pnpm restaurant:create. The panel says that on the page, where the person holding the token can read it.

tokens.css · WCAG 1.4.3

The brand half is made of type

No photograph, no <img>, no gradient — a rendered guest check instead, because it says "restaurant point of sale" in one glance and costs nothing to ship. It also has one ink: --c-accent-ink is the only pair tokens.css guarantees on --c-accent, at 6.13:1, so every level of hierarchy there is size, weight or tracking and never a faded colour.

invariant 1 · the money module

A specimen, never a figure

No dashboard surface in this product prints a money figure yet — not revenue, not today's takings, not a zero, because a zero and a broken query look identical. The slips here are drawn from sample 04's menu with their arithmetic done by hand, and each caption says so. When real numbers arrive they will come from lib/server/money, which owns the one rounding rule.
