Field study · counter POS

The layout grammar of a quick-service POS

What NCR and Oracle actually publish about designing a till screen — and which of it survives contact with a dine-in restaurant that has tables, rounds of ordering and split bills.

There is no single “KFC POS.” KFC runs different stacks by market and franchisee. Yum!’s own platform, Byte by Yum! (formerly Poseidon), was in roughly 7,000 restaurants across all four brands at the end of 2025 — against KFC’s 30,000+ international restaurants alone — and management says KFC “requires expanded deployment over time.” No Byte interface screenshots have been published. The useful reference isn’t a photo of a KFC till; it’s the design manuals its vendors publish openly.

Zone by zone

What the manuals say, and what you should do

ZoneWhat NCR & Oracle publishFor matcami
1Status bar Simphony carries a four-panel status bar. Offline state is a permanently visible two-part indicator in the chrome — never a toast. Toast goes further: a full-width yellow banner after ~40s, plus an explicit list of what still works. Adopt
Spec §6 already mandates the unsynced count always on screen. The vendors independently arrived at the same rule — that’s confirmation, not coincidence. Go further than Simphony’s two small text labels.
2Function rail Aloha reserves a fixed region for standard buttons whose size and position cannot be moved. Destructive functions sit with the rest. Adapt
Keep the rail, but separate the seven owner-PIN actions visually. They must be fast to reach deliberately and hard to hit by accident — opposing forces the QSR layout never had to resolve.
3Category tabs Categories are reached by tabs, not drill-down-and-back. Simphony’s tabbed template caps near 50 tabs and 50 buttons per tab. Oracle pulls buttons in via Screen Lookups that auto-group, sort and paginate — menu items are not hand-placed. Adopt
Tabs, and generate the grid from the menu snapshot rather than positioning buttons by hand. A hand-placed grid is a second source of truth that drifts from §5’s menu version.
4Item grid MICROS’s fast-food layouts ran 35 and 54 keys. Aloha exposes grid geometry as absolute pixels — rows, columns, per-button size and spacing — hand-tuned, not fluid. Vendor advice: most-ordered items on the default panel. Adapt
Absolute-pixel grids assume a fixed terminal. A browser app on unknown hardware can’t. Pin a target resolution, then verify the touch target in mm, not px.
5Guest check The check pane is permanent; everything else swaps around it. Stated rationale: the operator must never navigate away to see what’s been rung in. Modals are for exceptions only — errors, reason codes, approvals. Adopt
The single most transferable rule on this page. Aloha encodes void-vs-delete by item text colour alone — don’t. WCAG 1.4.1, and 1 in 12 men has red-green CVD. Carry a glyph too.
6Totals Discount, tax and total stacked directly above tender, monospaced and right-aligned for column scanning. Adopt
One formatter, integer cents in and a string out. §17’s “one rounding rule in one function” is a UI rule as much as a domain one — the screen must never round independently.
7Tender Called out as the highest-value real estate on the screen; the target is one-touch close. Quick tenders come in two documented flavours: exact amount due, and next-highest-dollar. Adopt
Cheap to build, disproportionately loved. But card and mobile can’t auto-complete offline (§6) — those tenders must visibly disable, not fail after the tap.
Kitchen Display System — every vendor assumes a KDS receives the order. Reject
Explicitly on the do-not-build list. Kitchen tickets print to ESC/POS. Don’t let a borrowed pattern smuggle a KDS into the MVP.
Hard numbers

What to build against

20 mm Touch target from the standing-kiosk literature — roughly 111 CSS px, about 2.5× Apple’s 44pt. kiosk human-factors · px conversion is arithmetic, not a published figure
9.2 mm Research-backed floor for accurate finger use. Error rate falls with size up to ~10mm, then flattens. touch-target research
500 nits Real counter hardware, with anti-glare coating and optical bonding. A browser app on a generic tablet will be dimmer than what it replaces. POS hardware specs
85 dBA Counter and kitchen noise routinely hits the occupational action level. Audio can never be the primary confirmation channel. occupational noise standards
1 in 12 Men with red-green colour vision deficiency — which rules out colour alone for the free/occupied and NEW/SENT/VOIDED states. WCAG SC 1.4.1, Level A
32 / 48 Characters per line on thermal receipts. A separate design problem from the screen: no colour, no images, COPY on reprints. ESC/POS · spec §11
The gap

Four things no QSR layout solves

A counter till closes one transaction at a time. Yours doesn’t — and this is exactly where the borrowed grammar runs out.

Spec §13

The check is long-lived

QSR: ring, pay, clear. Yours: a table opens, items arrive in rounds over an hour, the bill prints, more items land and re-open it. OPEN → BILLED → PAID needs to read at a glance — and BILLED has no QSR equivalent at all.

Spec §13

A table map is a primary screen

No QSR reference has one. Free vs occupied plus open amount, transfer and merge — a whole screen the manuals are silent on. Its state colours need the same non-colour redundancy as the check.

Spec §13

Split and merge bills

Split by item, split equally, split a payment across methods. Direct manipulation of a check that already exists — nothing in the QSR grammar covers it, and it’s where mis-taps cost real money.

Spec §8 · §14

Owner approval, done properly

Aloha signals a persistent “Manager Mode” with a green border around the whole screen — worth stealing. But Simphony lets the void reason code be switched off entirely. §14 makes it mandatory. Don’t inherit that flexibility.

From operators, not vendors

What actually goes wrong

  • 01Taps are the unit of measure. Operators count added taps, not seconds, and adding one to a checkout flow is the single most-hated change they report. Sub-second lag between item adds is described as system failure during a rush.
  • 02Errors cluster on modifiers, not items. In one 2025 drive-thru study, roughly half of all order errors were a single missed modifier. Your recipe deductions ride on modifiers too — so a mis-tap there corrupts stock, not just the plate.
  • 03Shared manager PINs are the normal failure. Approval still happens; it just stops identifying anyone. Your audit row stores the approver — which only means something if each person has their own PIN and nobody lends it.
  • 04Gloves and grease are permanent, not occasional. The FDA Food Code bans bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat food, so a till shared with food handling is operated gloved. Practitioners note grease on capacitive glass even shortly after handwashing.
  • 05Unannounced layout changes are treated as an incident. Staff get retrained mid-shift; tiles shrink below usable. A menu edit that reflows the grid during service is the same event — §5’s version bump has a human cost.
  • 06Cloud dependence takes the drawer down too. McDonald’s closed restaurants worldwide for hours in March 2024 over a third-party config change. Your offline-first design is the right call; this is what it’s insuring against.
  • 07Steal Simphony’s battery interlock. Below 5% it blocks opening a check entirely, warns at 15%, logs both, and the warning can’t be dismissed. A tablet dying mid-order with unsynced operations is your worst case — and it’s preventable in the UI.
